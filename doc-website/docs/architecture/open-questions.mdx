## 🟡 How much cache memory the engine needs

ElastiCache Serverless for Valkey was chosen ([ADR-03](./decisions.mdx#adr-03)), but its memory is the estimate we trust the least:

| What | Estimate | Depends on |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Idempotency | 18 MB (1 h TTL) to 430 MB (24 h TTL) | The TTL |
| 10–30 min windows | ~15 MB | Accounts active in the window |
| Account history (`out:` 24 h, `in:` 60 min) | ~260 MB of entries plus ~100 MB of keys | Measured at ~70 bytes per entry in Valkey: ~3.7 million live outgoing entries at 50 tx/s. Replaces the earlier 100–200 MB of 24-hour sums and ~100 MB of velocity history |
| Accounts and merchants (`acct:`, `merch:`, HU-67) | **~605 MB, measured** | ~261 bytes per account hash (currency, owner id, document, status, with its TTL) × 2.3 million accounts, plus ~1 MB for 5,000 merchants. Measured on 2026-11-02 by loading 200 thousand synthetic accounts with the cache loader into a local Valkey 7 and extrapolating to the full-mode seed |
| Restricted list cache (`{restricted}:docs`) | **~3.3 MB, measured** | 50 thousand documents in one set |
| Active rules | A few KB | — |

With the account history and the reference data of HU-67 the cache would be **~1 GB**. At $0.084 per GB-hour that is about $61 a month of memory, some $40 more than the ~$23 of the cost table (which assumes 300 MB); the cost table does not change until the load test confirms the history part. Ways to bring it down if needed: keep only the accounts with recent activity, or drop `user_id` from `acct:` (the engine does not read it today). Still open: the load test runs did not record it. [Load test the engine](../getting-started/load-test.mdx) says how to read the cache's `BytesUsedForCache` and `ElastiCacheProcessingUnits` after the 300 tx/s step, which closes this question. Also pending: the real latency of the serverless layer and the operations it consumes per transaction.

## 🔵 Cases consumer: Lambda or Fargate

All three queues are consumed by Lambda. In the AWS run of November 11 each case was open 0.4 to 0.6 s after its decision, with no errors ([AWS run](../components/aws-run.mdx)). The time per batch at production volume is not measured yet: if case processing turned out heavier, that consumer can move to Fargate (~$30/month per task).

## ✅ Real duration of the two Spark jobs

Both run every night on AWS Glue 6.0 with two G.1X workers ($0.308 per DPU-hour for Glue 6.0, billed per second, 1-minute minimum). The cost table assumes 10 minutes each, about $6.16 a month. In dev the whole nightly machine (reading the files, both Glue jobs, the differences, the export and the views) took 7 min 33 s on November 5, so each job takes less than the 10 minutes assumed and the estimate is on the safe side.

## ✅ EKS against Fargate for the decision engine

Answered in [ADR-06](./decisions.mdx#adr-06) on November 10: the engine ran on Kubernetes locally with kind, with the same image and variables, and the comparison covers latency, operation and cost (Fargate $72.08 against about $134 a month on EKS). EKS itself was not deployed because it is not on the account's AWS free plan.

## ❗ p99 above 200 ms at 300 tx/s with 10 tasks {/* #p99-at-300 */}

RNF-01 and CA-09 ask for a decision p99 of 200 ms or less at 300 tx/s. The load test meets it at 50 and 150 tx/s, and at 300 tx/s in most minutes, on the client's p99 and with 20 tasks. With 10 tasks of 0.5 vCPU it is **not met end to end**: the load balancer's p99 goes above 200 ms in some minutes (run 5: 229 ms over the step; runs 7 and 8: 205 to 215 ms). The cause is an engine task pausing 100 to 300 ms about once a minute, not the CPU or the cache. Not proven: blocking log writes to CloudWatch or garbage collector pauses. Next check, after the delivery (team decision of 2026-11-08): the `awslogs` driver in non-blocking mode with `uvicorn --no-access-log`, and the GC pauses measured in the same run. Details in [Load test the engine](../getting-started/load-test.mdx#known-issue).

## 🟡 Where the Kaggle history lives in production

In dev (MAG-312) the full CSVs are in `raw/kaggle/` and the seed's Kaggle ETL reads them from there, so AWS no longer needs a local copy. Today the PaySim and Sparkov sample linked to the seed users is in the PostgreSQL `transacciones` table (loaded once, never edited), and the full CSVs are in `raw/kaggle/` in S3. Still to decide whether in production the sample also moves to S3 (and DynamoDB), so that all the history is in one place. Jorge sees S3 as the more feasible option (2026-10-19); for now it stays as it is.
